\section{Introduction}

Cellular automata are complex systems: functions that define behaviors that are often chaotic but at other times easily predictable. Many studies have proposed classifications to tell different behaviors apart, such as Wolfram's classification, TODO, or the set of life-like rules. These classifications are usually designed according to behavior, but how similar are the rules within them? And how different are they from the rules of another set?

At present, classifications are the only measure we can use to decide whether two rules are alike, apart from directly comparing their exact behavior. However, there is no measure that tells us how similar they are. The purpose of this research is to define a method to compare the behavior of any two rules and measure their degree of similarity.

To study these similarities, it is first necessary to identify which exact relations already exist between different domains, find the equivalences, and use them as a reference metric for the proposed algorithms, which essentially reduce to computing spectral moments together with a method that captures the growth of the attractor mass.
