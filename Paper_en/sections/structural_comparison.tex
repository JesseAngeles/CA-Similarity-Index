\section{Structural comparison of transition graphs}
\label{sec:structural}

The previous relations depend on the rule vector. As an alternative, we compare the behavior: on a periodic space of $n$ cells, with $N = |\mathcal{S}|^n$ configurations, we evolve each configuration to obtain the transition graph $G_F$ of the rule. As a parallel method, we consider all the translations of a configuration as a single node (Section~\ref{subsec:quotient}), which yields a quotient graph with approximately $|\mathcal{S}|^n / n$ nodes. We then say that two rules have the same behavior if their transition graphs are isomorphic.

The isomorphism comparison confirms that the ECA rules related by state permutation or mirror have isomorphic graphs, which corroborates that the transition graph represents the similarity between rules. Since this process is computationally expensive, we use the canonical form of the graph: a topological encoding that does not depend on the node labels, so that two graphs are isomorphic if and only if their canonical forms are equal.

These methods have some restrictions: the comparison is binary, it is between rules of the same domain, and the result depends on the size $n$ of the space (Figure~\ref{fig:all-methods}). In ECA, the full graph distinguishes 82 classes with 4 cells and a maximum of 85 with 6. From $n = 8$ on, some patterns appear: even $n$ groups \{15,170\}, \{23,178\} and \{77,232\}; $n$ multiple of 4 also groups \{105,150\}; odd $n$ groups \{10,34\}, \{24,46\} and \{136,160\}; $n = 7$ and $n = 23$ group \{60,90\}. Combining an even and an odd size greater than 5 yields the 88 classes. The quotient distinguishes 83 classes with 6 cells and a maximum of 85 with 10; from $n = 7$ on, the number depends only on $n \bmod 4$ (80 classes if $n$ is odd, 85 if $n \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ and 84 if $4 \mid n$), and for every $n \ge 6$ it groups \{12,34\}, \{15,51\} and \{170,204\}.
